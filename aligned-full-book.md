# Aligned

*Lead with Love, Live with Intention*

by Blake Cody

## Chapter 1: Grieving a Ghost, The Person You're Mourning Never Existed

You hear the dreaded words; your stomach falls through the floor. Your chest tightens; you stop listening, trying to process what is actually happening. Everything in your body becomes hollow, and you feel the tears rushing to exit your eyes. While tears fall, everything in your body loses energy, and it feels like the world just came crashing down right on top of you. The hardest part of a relationship. When we get into them, we never think about the inevitable until it arrives. The person you are imagining a future with and ready to walk through life with has just slipped through your fingers. The heartbreak is terrible, and it's not a bad thing if you have to grieve to heal yourself back up into the person you were the day before the breakup. Healing is a good part of heartbreak because it gives you many lessons throughout your life, and everything happens for a reason. Some people are heartbroken over someone whom they only met for a month, or never even made a move. Whether it was on a dating app, and you thought the date was going smoothly. Then one day, out of nowhere, you never hear a text back from them. We can be heartbroken when someone we like gets into another relationship, but in reality, if they get into a relationship with someone else in your fingertips, they were not looking at you as an option or not the right one in the moment. So we grieve. Through lonely nights, where you sit alone, playing back all the memories that you once had with them. If it were someone you had been talking to for one month, maybe you are playing your future repeatedly in a what-if scenario. Heartbreak is hard to control because emotions can take over, and the text that you shouldn't have sent is now in their phone. Having the mindset of "I don't care anymore about this person," but if you're texting them, then you are casting your last hook for attention.

I can't just type this page and say that heartbreak is not something you should cry over. The void is now just sitting in your life, and your life has just been completely flipped upside down. Imagine you are running a race, and then suddenly you roll your ankle and you can't run anymore. Like relationships, you are running through life with this person, and one step later, they are not even there anymore. You cannot continue the race until you heal your ankle. With one foot, you can slowly proceed to completing the race, which is what people with heartbreak do; they make it through long days even if they have little to give, until they heal again. When we think of heartbreak, what do we normally think about?

I want you to think about that question for a minute. Heartbreak and grieving over the loss of someone is what we normally think of. When we grieve over the heartbreak, we are actually grieving over the thing that we thought it was. The partner that you thought would be your first kid's mother/father, obviously, now you know this person will not be that. When we are grieving, we normally don't grieve over the person, but the many experiences of how they made us feel, and now knowing what we thought was going to be a lifetime is now gone in the snap of a finger. The person you got to know for one month made you feel special. You saw some spark, and now you are dreaming about your future together. Then they ghost you. That heartbreak is grieving over what you thought was going to be, so understand that we are not grieving over the loss of the person. You're not missing them, you're missing the version you made in your head. You're not missing them, you're missing the future you built inside your own mind. You're not missing them, you're missing the way you felt thinking it was true love. You're not missing them, you're missing the fantasy of love you thought you were in. Remember, heartbreak will take time, some longer than others, but understand what you are actually heartbroken over. Most of the time, it's not the person; it's the version you created in your head. In reality, it was never them.

How are we able to navigate through a breakup? The initial shock when the other person mentions they want to go their separate ways. There are many people who react to this differently. Some people try to plead their case to keep the relationship intact. Which I think is a terrible idea. The other person is walking out the door of your life, so what makes you think they are going to stay even longer, and if they do, a lifetime? We think of relationships as two people choosing each other. They chose you at one moment, but now are trying to take back their decision. They are not choosing you anymore as someone they want to fight for in their life. The priorities they have changed. When they stopped fighting for your love is the biggest red flag waving through the air. They didn't decide five minutes before the conversation; they decided through constant fights in their own head on whether or not they wanted to stay. Let them walk out of your life; they're already one foot out the door. Don't fight for something that you think you want. Then we get those who are so heartbroken that they decide to text the other person, maybe after a couple of hours. Trying to get that final say to see if they can come back into your life. This isn't a moment where you are missing the person who just walked out of your life; this is you fearing what loneliness is going to feel like. You don't want to go back into a world where you don't have someone by your side. So you do whatever it takes to keep this person. You are acting solely on emotions and fear in this situation and not letting yourself fully embrace the reality that is about to set in: loneliness. This phase will be the hardest part to emotionally overcome during the heartbreak: the first couple of hours, or that first night where you wake up and it still doesn't feel real, but it is. You are now in a world where you are single. Getting through that initial shock will be the hardest part; your nervous system doesn't want the change, but once it fully accepts it, then the remaining healing will be a little easier. Now, we are past the initial shock, and this person is officially gone. The void is now entering their lives.

The main thing I realized during this time was the void it caused me. Throughout a relationship, you have this piece that you offer to them. Texting how they are, hanging out with the person, telling them all your jokes, and telling them the drama at work between two people. When a relationship ends, it leaves you with a large void that is no longer filled by your partner. Who can you vent to, tell your crazy conspiracy theories to, or just lay down after a long day and watch a movie with? All those moments were filling a piece of you. The strange part is that your partner had quietly become most of your daily life. Now you have to navigate without someone you solely relied on each day. You now feel days are longer than ever; your phone texts are officially done for, and your day feels very uneventful. It's only you now. Trying to fill in this void was the hardest part, and although today I fill that void with some other things, just like this passage I am writing, I still have some of me that is ready to be filled. Am I leaving it open just in case I can find someone someday, maybe?

Coping with the void in your life is hard. First, accept the fact that this person is not the right choice for you. You may have put them so high in your life, but let me ask you a question. How can someone who does not choose you be the right person for you? It's a great question to ask because they choose you at some point, and they made you feel seen and heard, but as time slowly faded, the affection that they once saw in you is drifting away into the sunset until one day the sun sets. The question is the one I always ask people going through a breakup. They thought it was something different; they realized it's not. Now, they are acting on their gut. Accepting the outcome of their choice is the first step in filling the void because you can't heal if you are constantly stuck in the past. You must face reality and realize that they are not who we made them out to be. Once you accept this, then you can start filling your cup.

I remember seeing this video of a professor taking a bucket and filling it with golf balls. He asked the class, "Is this bucket full?" Obviously, the class said yes because they were thinking of adding another golf ball into the bucket. Then the professor took out some marbles and filled the bucket with more stuff. "Is this bucket full?" he asks again, repeatedly. He keeps doing this with rocks, sand, water, and whatever fits into the bucket. Think of your life as this bucket with golf balls, marbles, rocks, etc. Let's say we decide to put the sand in first. Then the rocks, then so on and so forth. We won't be able to fit as many golf balls or marbles into this bucket. We want to adjust our lives in order to fit as much as we can into our bucket. The golf balls are the big things in life. Our family, dreams, etc. and everything else are the smaller things: your hobbies and activities. Finding the balance of friends, family, hobbies, and everyday activities is important. Having a good balance will help us overcome the void that might hit in our lives. Leaving us to a happier and more fulfilled life rather than one that can feel repetitive and boring. At the beginning of a breakup, we will see a void in our life that is wide open, and you can do anything you want within this unknown part of your life. Are you planning to use this extra time you now have to do something you love and that brings you happiness, or keep thinking of hypothetical scenarios, beating yourself up because you are not enough?

When people break up, this is normally the first thing they ask themselves. How was I not enough for this person? What did I do that caused them to think this way? These things will go through the person's head. In reality, the person who broke up with the other had already decided days or months before it actually happened, and just didn't communicate what was getting them to think this way. Sometimes the relationship actually just won't work out, and one side of the party realizes that. Trying to imagine a future, and your current boyfriend or girlfriend isn't even in the image. The problem here isn't anything the person did, but just the vibe and compatibility aren't the same. Then the breakup happens and leaves the person spiraling about how they are not enough.

It's tough when this happens because one day you think everything is going well. You're planning your future with this person, planning what you want to eat for dinner, where you want the wedding to be, and these future scenarios. Only for it all to come crashing down in just one conversation. Feels like someone just dropped a grenade on your whole life and just walked away from everything. Why getting broken up with is hard: not only do you get this void, you don't get the closure that you may have needed. You ask yourself who you are and why you weren't enough. Then, through it all, you have no one to answer the questions for you; all the answers are within your own mind. We know the answers we form are never the actual answers; it's some over-thought scenario that played in our head. Sometimes you can't even get the answers, or you get too many answers, so you can't decide which one to stick with. During this time, it is very easy to spiral out and lose all desire for a relationship.

The way we talk to ourselves can be hard. We see our every flaw, our every decision, and we live in our own heads 24/7. The only person you will be with from the day you were born to the day you pass away is yourself. All of us are sitting through the lonely hours, the good times, the panic attacks, and the constant battles that play in your own head. Perhaps people always label you as the one with a bright smile who cares for others. When deep down you know the battles and moments that shape you into who you are. You know yourself better than anyone can see from the outside. Building that inner self-awareness can be a challenging and long process, but in the end, it is much needed for self-love. I will go into more about this later.

Many people in life will have periods where they may have felt lonely. The only thing that it seems they want is a relationship with a person. Moments like these could go one of two ways. The person can sit there and understand the genuine emotions that they are going through. Find out what they love, how they are hard to handle, what type of boundaries they want in their next relationship, and many other traits they could ask themselves. As in almost anything, there are two sides to a coin. Some people will rush into their next relationship for the fact of not embracing loneliness, running from their own thoughts and emotions. They have someone who can fill that void in their life. Watch out for these people when looking for your next partner. If someone is too scared to live by themselves, then how can they be capable of another relationship? For me, I have been trying to figure out all the emotions and habits that could make me a better partner in my next relationship.

Now that we are alone, we have ourselves to depend on. It's a tough spot to be in because it feels like everyone around you is just going about their day while you sit there fighting yourself about where you're currently at in your life. You're one of the roughly 1 in 5 people who feel this way; you are not the exception you think you are. Everyone has to go through this phase; some build themselves back up, some sit with their feelings and understand them, and some people will rush into another relationship for the cheap dopamine. The last point is the thing I do not want you to do. Do not get into another relationship until you know you are ready yourself. If you fear being alone and you are going into a relationship based on that fear, research tells us a couple of things that happen. The first thing is that you settle for less than you deserve. People think this person in front of them is the only person who will come into their life, or maybe even this other person is way out of their league. So they settle. The second thing that happens when you enter relationships in this stage is that you become dependent on them out of fear of being alone. You start to not be yourself in the relationship and instead start doing what this other person wants you to do, people-pleasing. Think of someone trying to ride a bike. The person will never let you take the training wheels off, and that is your life for the rest of time. This situation molds you to this person's standards instead of your own, which can later on in life affect your mental health. All of us in life want to set out and do things that excite us.

A couple of years or months down the road, this relationship is now what it didn't seem like it was when you were alone. Research indicates that you will fear leaving them, a third outcome of entering a relationship because of the fear of solitude. You're staying in this relationship for the same reason that makes you not want to end it. The fear of loneliness. The thought of being with this person is better than the thought of being alone. Imagine you are building a Lego set. You are already halfway done with the build when you realize you misplaced one piece, making the LEGO look dysfunctional and weird. Are you going to take everything apart and fix that one piece so it can become just like the picture on the box, or are you just going to leave it and let it look off? It's the same thing. Think of your love life as this LEGO. You want it to be just like the picture on the box, but you need to put in the work to break it all down and restart. Even though you've made so much progress on the build, there is no chance that you can complete the structure to look correct unless you restart. To do it all over again, but this time building the structure right. When going through this stage of your love life, remember that loneliness is a scary place to be, but we must go back into the unknown and understand who we are before we can finish the puzzle the correct way.

I want to share with you some things I learned about myself throughout this time. First things first, when I first had my breakup, I had a moment where I wanted to show this person what they were missing. Going to the gym seven times a week, trying to gain as much muscle mass as possible, and trying to run every day. I started the 75 Hard challenge a day after the breakup. If you don't know what that is, it's a workout program that transforms your current self in the matter of 75 days. I was the one who broke up with them. I was the one who wasn't ready for a relationship, and I acted like someone had just broken up with me. Basically, I'm trying to be the next David Goggins. But in reality, this had its own downfall. I never enjoyed the stuff; I just had that motivation that pushed me through the early workouts and the two-a-days I was doing in the gym until my tank ran empty. Many people will go through this phase right after a breakup. It's normal. You have that motivation that can push you farther than you thought you could; I say you should use that. With this motivation, you try to show the other person who you can be, rather than being that person before the relationship broke up. This mindset will soon fizzle out. A big opening point for who I was becoming was this breakup.

Everyone is going to have a different timeline with heartbreak. Maybe it was someone you spent half your life with; getting over that is not just going to take two days. Much of our life has now changed, and we are adapting to what life currently is, not what we thought it was. Don't think that there is a certain timeline for getting over a heartbreak. That doesn't mean obsessing over them for months and months, but just trying little things to slowly guide you back to the version of yourself that didn't rely on this other person. Some days it might be hard to get out of bed, but you get up anyway. That is one step closer to getting your spark back. When you are healing, the healing process might seem complete when you are no longer ever thinking about them anymore. That is wrong. You will know you are healed when you can think about this person and control your emotions about them. Not going through a constant spiral, or getting fueled up with anger because you hate this person. It could be something like seeing a billboard they pointed out, their favorite restaurant that they always raved about, or even their favorite song in the car that plays. You once loved someone who was attached to these things. Just pretending that they no longer exist is running away from your emotions, instead of facing them head-on. You never heal that way. Understanding what emotions flow through your body when they pop up is what will get you to heal from the void they put into your life. Over time, you will start labeling things you once loved with other events that may have happened, but until you can do that, they will always pop up, and we have to learn how to control ourselves when these moments spark.

Now you are learning how to heal, starting healing, or maybe even being fully healed from your past. I say that once you heal, a very dangerous moment unfolds right in front of your eyes. The term we all use is loneliness. The art of being alone with no one to talk to and fill our lives. Even if you absolutely love yourself, we are humans who crave interaction. We have friends, family, or other people who can fill that void, but they can only get you so far. Having a wonderful system of friends behind you makes this stage bearable because you have an outlet when times get tough, someone to talk to when you're feeling lonely. If you have someone like this in your life, make sure they understand how much they mean to you, because it is rare to get someone who you are comfortable enough to let out your emotions, and they can sit there and understand everything you are going through. No matter how close and emotionally connected you are with a friend or family you will still have those moments where loneliness sets in. The notifications that you were used to every day are now nonexistent, the funny joke that you heard at work you now have no one to tell, or the stressful day that you had at work just sits inside you, and you can't tell your partner about. Your world is empty at some parts of the day. You have healed from your past, and now you have no one in this current moment of your life, so it is very easy to get attached to someone too quickly who shows you the smallest amount of attention. That craving was never really about them. It was that voice inside your head; the one that got louder the second they walked away, telling you maybe you weren't enough after all. You don't quiet that voice by replacing the person. You quiet it by becoming someone you'd actually choose.

## Chapter 2: The Formula, Becoming Someone Worth Choosing

Your internal voice doesn't leave when the heartbreak does. It sits all cozy in the back of your mind, screaming that you are not enough. No one is going to accept you for who you are; why would they? After heartbreak, this voice can haunt our minds, leaving us to question ourselves whether we are enough. "Who would want to date someone like us" is the mindset we accept. But what if that voice was completely wrong? The voice that tells you you're one flat, empty thing with nothing to offer. That's the lie. You were never one thing. You're a stack of traits: the way you make people laugh, the way you show up. It's never one trait that makes someone impossible to replace. It's the combination of them. Your unique pairings.

Unique pairings are crucial for a relationship because they separate you from the rest of the field. Most of us have the same type, but have a different formula for our unique pairings. A couple of unique pairings could be funny, getting the woman or man to laugh at jokes that you can slide into normal conversations. Being intelligent could be another one. When you think of someone and dwell on losing them, you normally don't think "They were so good in bed," it might be "They were so good in bed, and I loved how they made me laugh all the time" or "They were so good in bed, and I loved the way they made me feel seen and listened when I was speaking about my drama." The "and" in this sentence is a perfect representation of the unique pairing we forget. The most important pairing I put at the top of the list is respect. And respect differs from being liked; most people never stop to consider that difference. Being respected means someone looks at your values, your beliefs, your character, everything you've built, and honors it. They don't want to change you. They take your flaws and understand them. Being liked is different. Someone can enjoy your company but quietly wish you were a little more ambitious, a little more reserved, a little more of something you're not. They like the version of you they spend time with. They'd just edit a few chapters. Respect means they want the complete book.

These unique pairings are important in standing out in the crowd of people. When you think about your love life, start trying to understand some unique pairings that you can bring to the table. If you're in the dating scene, you hear the same conversations at the bar every single night. "What do you do for work?" "How are you doing today?" "What are you doing here alone?" Everyone hears those lines, so everyone's brain runs them on autopilot. The nervous system has heard them a thousand times and tuned them out. Tired lines don't make you stand out. They make you blend in. Your unique pairings are what break the autopilot. The more unique pairings you have, the brighter you look. The more "ands" you carry, the harder you become to walk away from. Building your unique pairings doesn't just make you a better partner; it makes you magnetic. The dating coach Matthew Hussey talks about becoming rare and valuable, being the kind of person who draws people in instead of chasing them. I think of it as a flame and a moth. The flame lights up a room. It draws people in naturally. The moth chases the light desperately, only to be consumed by it. Your unique pairings make you the flame. Building up your humor, depth, character, and respect leads to a brighter inner glow. The moth has one thing going for it. The flame has everything. And everyone in the room feels it.

Knowing your unique pairings and understanding the flame and the moth is one thing. Actually getting on the court is another thing. A coach can draw up the perfect play, but the player still has to execute it. I am living proof of this. I have read the books, watched the videos, studied the methods, and still stand on the sidelines. Knowing exactly what to do and actually doing it are two completely different things. Everyone around you will say the same thing: "Just go talk to them. What's the worst that can happen, they say no?" And as frustrating as that advice is to hear, they're right. Although it's not the easiest thing in our lives. So, how can we find the right person for ourselves? When we think about who we want as a person, let's say you want one who works on themselves and is more of a homebody, because that is what you are too. Are you going to find this person at the bar at 2 am? Maybe. They are out for one night, having a grand time, but most likely they are outgoing people who are always going to go out. We want to find scenarios and areas where we might meet these people. Maybe that place is in a Pilates class; we can see a bunch of single people in a Pilates class trying to meet more people. We can see some of these people at the gym. Maybe you sign up for running clubs in the community, or volunteer for some work to help the community. These different scenarios will allow you to meet new people who have the same interests in life as you. When we assert ourselves in situations like these, we can make new friends. In communities like these, I am not saying you are going to find the person for sure. We are expanding into different communities. When you meet a new friend, you are expanding your own community by welcoming theirs into your own life. You have your current community, and when you make friends, you are now being introduced to a whole chamber of different people, their community, and who they know. Every new community is a door and behind it are people who know people you've never met. Your person might be three doors deep.

I see a lot of times people are joining or going to these said communities, and they are still getting nothing. They are going to the community and thinking that since they are there, people will talk to them. That is how it should work, right? Well, that is completely wrong. The mistake most people make is showing up and staying closed off; headphones in, eyes down, body language that says don't come near me. Let's use the gym as an example, because I can relate. When I am at the gym, I am open to talking to whoever, but I still never get approached or talked to. I always questioned myself why that is, especially when I look over and see the regulars who are there every day just talking with one another. I thought to myself, "I am here every day; how come no one is talking to me?" It was because I wasn't approachable. Most people don't realize that most of our actual issues aren't that we are not good at approaching people, cause I sure am not. The actual issue is that we don't look approachable at all. I was sitting there with both headphones in my ears; if I had a hat on, I would move it down so you couldn't really see my face, and I was always on my phone in between sets. If I ever see someone look like that at the gym, I wouldn't even approach them. What made me think I was going to be approached at all? We want our character to look approachable. The concept I want to introduce here is simple: green lights. Not the ones on the road, but the ones in your dating life. A green light is a signal that tells the person across the room that you are friendly, open, and safe to approach. Like a stoplight, people move on green and stop on red. Most of us are unknowingly flashing red without realizing it. The moment I pulled one headphone out between sets, everything changed. I started meeting people. Regulars I now talk to every single day. It is so important to be in certain communities, but it is just as important to appear approachable in them, because if you are not, people won't feel warm enough to approach you. We overestimate how impressive you need to be, whether it's looks or some activity, and we underestimate how making people feel that warmth contributes to them approaching us. I want to give you one task: start adding green lights into your dating world. Create at least one green light moment a week. Whether it's pulling one headphone out at the gym, making eye contact with someone passing at work, or asking a stranger in a coffee shop what they're drinking. There are so many scenarios where a green light can exist. A green light looks different everywhere. Eye contact and a genuine smile. Asking someone what they're drinking. Leaving space in a conversation for someone to enter. Small signals. Massive impact.

Now go back to that voice from the start of the chapter. The one telling you that you are not enough, you can't be in a relationship. Look at everything we just walked through. The unique pairings are already built (the humor, the respect you carry, the way you make someone feel). Communities would be lucky to know you, and those green lights were never about becoming someone new; they were about letting people finally see who you already were. I didn't build a better person in these pages. I just pulled out the ones that were hiding. That voice was wrong; it always had been. The only thing standing between you and the person across the room isn't whether you're enough. It's whether you're willing to be seen.

## Chapter 3: Reject the Fear, Why Regret Is the Real Enemy

You see a person, and now your love signals are going off at a million beats per minute. Butterflies enter your stomach, your mind races thinking what to say to them, and the countless scenarios that could go wrong. You want to talk to them, but being rejected, looked at, and mocked all are taking center stage in your mind. You think about the what-ifs, how they will view you. The fear of rejection hits home for many people; it does for me. A terrible fear to have in the realm of dating. The fear of rejection holds a powerful place in our minds because when we get rejected, we feel powerless, vulnerable, or exposed to the world. If I tell a girl I like her and she rejects me, different scenarios of what the world will be like start replaying in my head, like a 24-hour television that never shuts off. My brain jumps to the worst one, but I know my scenarios are nothing like the actual thing. The world will keep spinning if I get rejected by someone that I find attractive. I think rejection actually helps people find love more quickly. Getting rejected at the first stage saves you months in a relationship that was never going to work anyway. Every no gets you one step closer to the one. I know this, and it is so much easier said than done. I can tell myself repeatedly that being rejected is fine; it's how the world works and how humans interact. If you get rejected, life will still be the same as the day before, maybe even better, because you have the clarity that this person doesn't like you. If you get rejected, know that you are the one taking action in your life. You want to get out there and find the one. If you live in constant fear of rejection, you are delaying the chance to ever find the one. The first person you try to talk to won't always be the one. Go through constant rejection because each time you get rejected; you are just throwing the rotten apple out of the bin. Eventually, you will pick the good apple, and the only way to get to the good apple is by cycling through all the rotten ones.

I heard this one phrase from Matthew Hussey in a podcast he was doing. He said, "If you decide that rejection is the scariest thing to you, you'll always avoid people. If you decide that regret is the scariest thing to you, then you'll go out there and talk to every single person and enjoy doing it." I heard this quote, and it is an absolute eye-opener for many. Those of us who have the fear of rejection will never find someone. We never give ourselves a chance. If we have the thought that regret is the scariest thing in our lives, then we will not care what other people think of us. Now the thought becomes, "If I don't talk to this person, then I am going to regret it. And that pain of regret will be far more painful than if they tell me to hit the road and find someone else." If I told you you'd reach your dream, and all you had to do was fail 30 times first, you'd be counting down every failure like it's New Year's Eve. We take this same aspect into our relationships. I can't tell you what number you're going to find your person. One thing I can tell you is that the more you try, the closer you are to the one. With this mindset, we can't be afraid to fail, to get rejected; we are reshaping our minds to see the good in rejection rather than the constant mockery we think will follow. You gave yourself many opportunities in life and did them all with no fear. Now you can finally find the one, rather than playing the mind games of what if?

Why do we hate rejection? All rejection comes down to is someone not wanting to date you. I can't sit here and give you all the details on why we hate rejection. Although I will say why I think people don't like rejection. When we are rejected, we have failed at something. No one likes to fail at a certain task, whether it's in a job or just trying to get the girl. Rejection is never something we want; obviously, we will not like it. We are going into the unknown of a world we thought was going to be different. Another reason for rejection is the thought of others. You think about what if they say no, how they will think about you? They will start making fun of you, thinking that you ever had a chance with the girl. In reality, people might chuckle at the fact, but no one is going to care after five minutes. It's the facts. The world will keep spinning. Rejection occurs when someone expresses how they feel, and the other person doesn't feel the same way. Why should we mock someone who is vulnerable? I go into vulnerability later on in the book, but I think taking the chance, telling them how you feel, is stronger than hiding behind the wall and never knowing. In the end, you caught feelings for the other person. Now you're expressing them out loud and not just in your brain. Instead of hiding behind the wall, you're showing your true strength and character. I think being rejected because you tried is far better than sitting in a constant state of what if.

Most people never even try. They look across the room, decide they don't have a chance, and reject themselves before anyone else can. The fear of rejection is one of the toughest things the human brain carries, and yet we all carry it. If you get rejected, the first one will sting. The fifth one will hurt. The tenth one will get you closer to the one. We just need to get past the first to make it to the tenth. Every rejection brings you one step closer and answers the question you were too afraid to ask. You won't sit in regret wondering what could have been.

Not everyone has a fear of rejection. Some people put themselves out there constantly and still get nothing back. That's a different hard. Let me talk from the bottom of my heart on this one. I know you are trying to find someone. You are constantly putting yourself out there, and it hasn't worked yet. But if you stop, then it 100% will not happen. You will face rejection at every stage of your life, and this rejection just has many stages. The longer the journey, the more beautiful the arrival. It's a struggle; I'm not saying it's easy. Some things in life are worth struggling for so that we enjoy them later on. You are cycling through the people who don't want to choose you, like I said earlier, the rotten apples are being picked from the bin. We just have to find the correct apple. And when you finally do, after all those hard days, all those rejections, all those moments you wanted to quit, that person is going to mean everything. If love came easy every time, it would mean nothing. The harder the hunt, the greater the trophy.

Next time you're talking to someone for whom you feel some attraction. Ask them for their number or their Instagram; let them know what you like about them, and flirt a little. They can easily shut you down, but just tell yourself how much you would've regretted it if they walked away and found someone else without you getting closure on whether or not they liked you. Then, when they shut you down, smile and keep walking. Rejection was never the enemy. Regret is.

## Chapter 4: Anxiously Attached, They're Not on Your Mind, You're on Their Hook

Fred asks this person to hang out, and they do. After the date, Fred is on his way home. Replaying that joke he said, the way she touched his arm. Now he is planning his next move, debating whether to text tonight or wait until tomorrow. Fred gets home and instead of relaxing he sits there checking if she is active, if she read his message, re reading the text he sent multiple times seeing if it sounded weird. It's been 3 hours since the date and he is telling himself, "What did I do wrong, why hasn't she said anything?"

Fred got broken up about six months ago and has been healing throughout the process. Trying to be happy with himself and regulating his emotions of missing the person he lost. He is ready to start another relationship. Now that he is more open, he talks to people not for relationships but just to make himself more extroverted, because in order to meet your partner, you must meet people first. While Fred is talking to this person, he becomes more emotionally attached. Every sentence just feels like more of a connection to the person. Fred now has someone whom he likes. Just from the normal conversations he has been having.

Fred has feelings for her and desires a relationship, but why? No sign told him she would be good for him. He doesn't even know if she is interested in him. The first red flag. He likes her so much he ignores every red flag. The mixed signals. The back-and-forth over what she actually wants. He starts changing himself just to get her to like him. This happens all the time, especially early on. When Fred was going through his healing phase, he never had communication with someone he liked; he was working on himself, trying to repair the foundation of his love life. When it was time to step into the arena, he clung onto the first person who showed him attention instead of understanding how he felt around this person. If his nervous system was really at peace, or if adrenaline built it up because she showed him attention. How anxiously attached people feel in relationships. Throughout their whole lives, they never felt the validation of being chosen. Whether that was through parents not showing their love, or however someone might have felt this. When someone finally starts talking to them, they feel a connection; they bypass all the boundaries that they may have set for themselves in private, ignore all the red flags that are swinging right in front of their face, and now their whole mood depends on how this person acts. When we go into every conversation with someone you're interested in, expecting to see if they like you or not, then you will never know who makes you feel at peace. You are constantly evaluating the person, trying to seek their validation, rather than let them be the person they are, and your nervous system will tell you if they are actually willing to choose you or playing nice.

Very tough situation to be in with Fred. Letting go of this feeling is nearly impossible. You're trying very hard to spend time with or even talk to this person. Everything starts with you. How come nothing starts with them? If the person wanted to hang out or even talk to you, they would. It is as simple as that. When you are burning all this energy on someone who doesn't even show an interest in you, and they are going about their day, not even a single thought of you until you text them. It is the exact cycle that anxious attached people go through. They are so attached to this person that they see all the red flags as grey flags.

Trying to avoid this is impossible. Even when you tell yourself, 'let's see what happens if you don't start the conversation.' Days feel like weeks. Your brain is screaming at you to find attention. The dopamine rush of a text is what it feeds off of. When you stop texting, there is no dopamine. When you see a video you want to send them, but you've told yourself to keep your distance, take a step back. You'll get your answer: watch if they grow toward you, or if you're watering something that was already dead. You are feeling them pull away ever so slowly; let them. If your presence really affected their life, they will fight for it. Taking this approach makes people leave your life, and it will hurt. But you can't keep watering a dead plant. Let them be a chapter in your story, but don't let them be your story. I go by the quote, "If they wanted to, they would." If they wanted to hang out, they would ask. If they wanted to text you, they would reach out and see how you are doing. If they wanted to keep talking to you, they wouldn't leave short, one-word answers, but end all of their sentences with an open topic. When you are talking to someone and you are catching feelings, take a step back. Examine the area around you. Are you getting attached because you don't like being alone, are you excited because someone is finally showing you attention you haven't had in so long, or is this person honestly giving your nervous system a rest? Are you more attracted to this person, or are you more attracted to the life you want to live?

One quote that I use for this is, "Fall in love with the man or woman, not the institution." Read that again. A lot of us who can get attached quickly need to understand this quote. We are choosing our partner for, hopefully, the rest of our lives. If we enter the next relationship because we don't want to be lonely, then we are falling in love with the institution. We see this other person with all the green flags, fun times, and we put them on this pedestal. First, love bombing could scare someone away. Everything that you built in private doesn't matter anymore. You want to give it all to them. We look for relationships, one where both people can look each other in the eyes and be the supporting cast. But they can't be the protagonist in your movie. Second, raising someone on a pedestal because of the emotion they showed you is dangerous. Once you're in the relationship that pedestal starts to lower. As it lowers, you start seeing the flaws you've been ignoring since day one. By then, you've already spent your time on them. Time you could've used to notice this before you ever committed. That would be falling in love with the institution. Which one would you rather have in your life? Being single or being in the wrong relationship. One where you don't see a future, where they don't share your values, and where you don't see them as the mother or father of your kids, but you are in a relationship! I know my answer. If you are single right now, know this is hopefully the last time you will ever be single for the rest of your life. Now, we obviously know that it may not be true because things could happen; you trust the wrong person, or the other person might not be compatible with you. That is okay. The end goal is hopefully that we will never be single again. Embrace this moment in your life; do things that you never thought you could do, talk with whoever you want to see if there is a connection, and understand that being single is better than being in the wrong relationship. Know your values, beliefs, and what you bring into a relationship. There is only one person who can fill the void, and that's you. Start finding someone who can take what you've built and make it better. The right person shows you in small, consistent moments. Pay attention, and you'll see them.

When you first start dating or talking to someone, your brain tells you to give this person everything that they want. Most of the time, we want this person to love us, so we decide to drop everything and start paying full attention to the other. It's a never-ending pit to get into. We do not want to get so attached to someone that we appear as if we need them to live. You lived, however many months alone; you had no one by your side to give your love to. Now you have this person who shows you attention, and you want to dump all of your values, dreams, boundaries, and everything that you have built in private right out the window. The right person will come along into your life and value what you find valuable in your life. In a relationship, both sides must have their own hobbies, their own values, because a relationship comprises two brains, two thoughts, two bodies, two unique experiences in life. When you drop everything you love, then you are slowly trying to make the relationship one brain, one body, which in the long run will not work. I was always told I might not have all the talent, so I need to work hard. Whether it was in sports, or the classroom. Younger me was programmed to have a good work ethic. This work ethic can hurt us. If we decide to pour that drive into a relationship, we overwhelm them. We want to keep our drive pointed at us, and give them pieces of us: our time, our attention, our encouragement. All of it.

So how do you actually break this cycle? It starts before you ever meet someone. It starts with knowing who you are and what you are building so that when someone walks into your life; you aren't handing them the remote control to your emotions. Having a purpose and sticking to it makes you more attractive. Your partner doesn't want all the attention in their life. They want you to help them in certain aspects while you can still do your own thing. Throughout the day, your focus is something larger than the two of you. It is more attractive. You've got to find the happy medium for the person you are dating. You can't be so obsessed with work or a hobby that they feel completely unseen. Especially at the beginning of a relationship. If you have this drive, this dream, a goal that you set to achieve, make sure you still look around at the important things in your life. Although your partner loves what you are doing, they still want to feel like they have you. Even in small moments of the day. It doesn't need to be three hours. Take some time to show your partner, the person you're talking to, or anyone some attention with intention. In the end, the people who will have your back even through all the hard times are those in your smaller circle. They are the ones to show why you care for them. Remember, we all have the space to be productive and creative. Find a purpose, then trek through life with that purpose. Don't overwhelm the other person or give them no attention. Instead, be there to give them a hand and understand how much the person needs attention for the day. It is far more attractive than never giving them a moment to themselves.

When you take the time to understand their values, how much attention they need, and whether they shut down your new ideas and goals or are honest with you about what it takes, then you will know if this person is right or wrong. Having this outlook is a great start to finding the person you want to be with for the rest of your life. Understand your attachment styles and try to decipher what the person on the other end is signaling to you. You will then get a gauge of people who are good for you, and those who are bad for you.

Have you ever had a moment when you thought the person you're seeing is the one you for sure want to be with? Most of us have. Wondering when you will see them again, trying to run into them, waiting for the text to come through your phone. Sometimes you wait hours, even days. Every time you hear a notification on your phone, you go to check if it is them, getting that rush of adrenaline only for it to be a spam text telling you they are running a site-wide sale. Your mood gets taken over by how this person gives you attention. When they show you the attention that you want, you're in the greatest mood. When they don't show you attention because they are busy or just not interested in you, then the self-doubt rolls in. We see this a lot, especially with people who have been single for some time. They fantasize about the future, how it's going to pan out. We see it because of the anxiously attached mindset they may have. Over long periods of being single and dealing with themselves, no one was ever there to show them attention. Now they have that little grasp of attention, and they want to skip straight to happily ever after. But it can't be, not yet. It's tough not to get stuck going back and forth in your own head about whether this person is giving you the time of day because they like you, or if they're being nice. That's how our brains work. We feed on the social aspects of our lives, and we get just enough to make an impact in our brains. Now, picture yourself putting in a lot of effort, questioning if this is the right person for you, and then one day, you successfully start a relationship with them.

I am not saying this is going to happen for all relationships, but what if they have the same communication tactics that you were fighting for? The constant fights in your head are not going to go away. You got in the driver's seat, and you are committed. Now, instead of questioning if this person likes you or not, you are now questioning if this person is here to stay or a onetime thing. The thought pattern stays the same, just the words of the thoughts end up changing. When you are looking for someone, and every day it feels like something different about their intentions, then pose that as a red flag yourself and be careful how far you want to go in this type of game you are playing with yourself. I'm not trying to tell you to run away from the relationship or the person. A conversation about that will set your standards on whether or not they like you, whether they want to change their communication style to better fit you, and overall, simplify things so you are not constantly in the fight-or-flight mindset. After the conversation is a good time to re-establish later on if it's still the right one. ## Chapter 5: The Blueprint, Four Stages, One Destination

At some point in your dating life, you've probably felt completely lost on where you actually stand with someone. That confusion has a map. It has four stages, and understanding them might be the most clarifying thing you do for your love life. Matthew Hussey taught me these four stages.

The first stage is a very simple stage. Admiration. Admiration is the first time you see someone, and you think they are pretty, funny, beautiful, or whatever you may think of the person. Maybe it's from seeing someone across the bar, and you want to go over and talk to them. It could also be on dating apps where you swipe right instead of left. Admiration doesn't need much explanation. You see someone you're attracted to, and that's it. The admiration stage is the easiest to pass through. We all find many people attractive. I could say you probably hit this stage at least once a day, which leads me to my next stage.

Mutual Attraction. This stage is enormous for the longevity of the relationship. This is where you reveal many more colors about yourself, and so does the other person you are trying to get into a relationship with. Mutual attraction can be a critical phase too, because most of the time, the mutual attraction gets taken over by how good-looking this other person is. You meet someone who is finally interested in you, and you get these dopamine spikes. As BoJack Horseman has said, "When you look at someone through rose-colored glasses, all the red flags just look like flags." Someone could be smart and funny during this phase, but you see their colors in how they treat other people, or even how they handle hard times. All other colors brush by you; you see the green flags, but miss the red flags. Understanding your values in this stage is important, and I think before you actually move into this stage, you must sit down with yourself and understand who you are, who you want to be, and who you want the mother of your children to be. During this stage, it's hard to know what to do. Do you buy her flowers, or do you play it cool and let her chase you? I've seen people argue both sides. Some say buying flowers shows you have a loving side. Others say focus on yourself, and if she's interested, she'll stay. I don't fully agree with either. If you want to actually get to know someone, drop your guard, show them who you really are, show them you're open. Not someone who is hard to get. Although our brains think that the scarcer something is, the more precious it is. You feel someone drift away from you, and your brain thinks they are important to you. If they are easy to get and open their arms and say, "here I am, I like you," our brains will question and think, "if it's that easy, I'll go look for more." All of it is completely confusing and hard to navigate. You should be yourself at this stage, stick to your hobbies, and do what you are doing every day. Don't lose what you love to do, just to show the other person affection, but don't turn completely cold and see if they start chasing you. This stage takes some time. Some may take longer than others. Slow down and be where your feet are. Ask yourself the hard questions: is your nervous system calm around them? Are you seeing this person adding to the life you want to live? Relationships are a lot more than just love. Asking these questions will tell you if this is the right person for you. After you have answered all these questions, you can proceed to the third step.

Jumping into the second and third stages can be quite confusing. You may think that the other person is the one. Maybe you are a woman, and you want the guy to progress things into a committed relationship. You two are having great conversations, enjoying many belly laughs. When the two of you are together, your nervous system feels at peace. You are ready to commit to a relationship, but it takes two for this stage. If the other person does not agree to be in a committed relationship, it is like building a sandcastle right next to the ocean. Every time you build the castle, signaling you are ready to commit to the relationship, a wave comes barreling in and knocks down the castle. The hard part of getting to this stage is that you could be in Stage 2 for the longest time. It could take months or even years because both of you need to commit. Two to Tango. If only one is ready to commit, then it is impossible to get to stage three.

Commitment. Commitment is when two people agree to be in a relationship. There you go, you finally got the title that you two were looking for. I like to think of people making a loop back to this step in relationships. The first time is when they date, committing to seeing just this person and no one else. When we commit to someone, we are not committing to finally having the perfect person in life. There will be people who pass by that you may think are way hotter than the person you currently have. We see it a lot in my generation. We have so much life to live, and we feel this spark with someone, so why would we want to keep continuing along the path of someone we may not vibe with? There's a question that never gets asked, but it plays in someone's head. The girl at the bar who we think has a connection with us, or a friend we like, only shows us their good side. Meanwhile, we see our partner with all their flaws. All the times they got mad at us, all the times they didn't do what we wanted. We start setting an expectation of who our partner should be, instead of living with who they actually are. The real question going through our head in these moments: would I be happier with her instead? Am I settling? But think about the honeymoon phase. We only saw our partner's good side once too. When you commit to a relationship, you are saying you commit to this person no matter who comes into your life; you have made your ultimate choice on who you want to be with. Once they are now in this stage of a committed relationship, we circle back when it is time to marry this person. You are now committing to living a full life with this one person. Making a vow that no matter what happens, through life's difficulties, the only thing that can separate you guys is death. Till death do us part. Many people act like the job is over once they've committed. You got what you were looking for, so now it's time to sit back and relax from all the work you have done. I like to disagree with this and say it's just the start. Relationships can be a lot of work, and when someone finally commits to you, then it's time to start showing why they shouldn't look back in the rearview mirror anymore.

Compatibility is the fourth stage. Yes, compatibility, where the leaves of our four-stage tree finally show. Compatibility brings everything together. You test how you can support each other at this stage. Through the difficulties and challenges, this stage shows us how crucial it is for lasting love. Throughout your life, you and your partner are going to go through different phases of your lives. One of you may thrive and enjoy every bit of life, while the other is stressing over work or a surgery that they need to get done. Being compatible through these times strengthens the bonds of the relationship, or picks at it slowly until it's enough. As I am writing this book, building my Substack, and trying to turn my passion into a business, there are nights when the laptop doesn't close until midnight. Weekends that get swallowed whole by ideas I have to get out of my head and onto a page. If I were in a relationship right now, my partner would have a choice: feel neglected and pull away, or understand that what I am building is part of who I am. Compatibility isn't the partner who never feels lonely during those long nights. It's the partner who trusts the process because they believe in you. And on my end, compatibility means never letting the work become an excuse to make them feel invisible. Your version might look completely different: a health scare, a career change, a loss. But the dynamic is always the same. So it takes patience from the one who is flying in their life and perseverance from the one who is in the dumps. Going through these challenges is tough in the beginning, but strengthens a relationship like no other. One of you will move faster in life than the other at times. But you must know you're still on the same team. Admiration gets your attention. Mutual attraction gets your heart. Commitment earns your loyalty. Compatibility gets your whole life. Just don't expect to ever fully arrive there. Every challenge you survive together is Stage 4. It never stops. Neither should you.

## Chapter 6: The Zone, Someone Always Wants More

You've been doing the math in your head for weeks. Every text, every hangout, every moment that felt like something. You've rearranged your schedule, chosen your words carefully, and stayed up replaying conversations looking for the sign that it's finally moving forward. Then, one moment changes everything. Not because anything dramatic happened, but because you suddenly see clearly that the signals you thought were there were never really there at all. Welcome to the friend zone. Nobody's favorite place to be. Have you ever been in a position where this might be the case? The person you like wants to be friends, when deep down you want a relationship. It's not fun because what action do you want to take? Stop being friends with this person who you genuinely have had great times with, or even live with the fact that this is only going as far as friends. I'm not a fan of either option, but in the world we live in, we have to choose one of the two.

Staying friends looks like this: you finally built up all the courage you've been fighting to ask them, "What are we?" Your heart is racing, your palms are getting sweaty, and the minute they take to think feels like a lifetime. Then they say they see nothing but a friend in you. Your heart shatters, and your emotions want to take over, but doing so will only make things worse. Living with this answer is not as bad as it may seem. The only person who has been fantasizing about the future with both of you is yourself. Now that you have a sure answer on what they see in you, it is time to move on. They don't value you as a potential person to build a future with; therefore, even in a relationship, it won't work. When I say moving on, I don't mean never talking to them again. You might distance yourself, give them less than before, and if you communicate that to them, it's a bonus. Instead of going ghost, let them know something like this. "I understand your perspective. While it's not the answer I was hoping for, it's good that I can finally stop the stress I place upon myself. Now I am okay with being just friends, but just know that I am going to need some time to heal from this." This response lets them know you are okay with their decision, but you are going to need some time to decompress everything that you have taken in. It may take weeks or even months to heal, but that is the first step to getting back on track with your own love life. Being in this position can be so emotionally challenging. How do you stop liking someone at the snap of a finger? Because they told you they don't want to escalate things any further doesn't mean you instantly lose all attraction for them. At this stage, realize if it is a possibility for you. Are you able to control yourself enough so that every time you see them, you don't emotionally spiral out of control and replay that moment in your head over and over? If you realize you can't control yourself when you are with them, then you may need to let go.

I caught myself in something like this recently, but I never got to the point where I expressed my feelings. I could tell the person wasn't interested in the feelings I was displaying. They didn't want to give the wrong signals. They wanted to remain friends. This was helpful because I saw we weren't thinking the same way. What I did next really changed how I look at being in this position. Let's think of it like the stock market: your goal is to invest your time (the money you earned) hoping to earn more time (or more money) one day. You wouldn't invest in something that looks completely lost, with no signs of strength. We need to look at our peace and our energy in the same way. When you invest your time and energy into someone and you realize they are not giving you the same signs back, even if you guys started as only friends, then you have every right to get up and walk away.

Let's flip the script. What if you were on the other end of this moment: someone confesses their feelings towards you, but you don't feel the same way? Most people handle this differently. You don't want to hurt them more than they already are. You know that you have to reject them because you have felt no affection towards them, so why would you betray yourself by pretending to feel something you don't? Some people have to go through this; some also want to keep the spark alive. You keep them around, playing into their flirtations because you enjoy the attention, knowing you have no intention of taking it further. That is one of the most selfish things to do. Be direct. Be honest. Tell them you have no intention of taking things further than friends at the moment. Don't leave the door cracked to be kind. A clean no is kinder than a maybe that never becomes a yes. Ask yourself the harder question. What signals did you send that made them feel the attention from you in the first place? It didn't come from nowhere. Understand where their mind went. It came from your actions.

The first person you need to look out for is yourself. You live your own life, and at the end of your story, you want to look back and say you lived a significant life. A significant life won't consist of you constantly going to battle with yourself because of the situations you put yourself in. You need to let go of the person, stop seeing them in your daily or weekly plans, until you heal yourself. Many people call this "being cold" or "cold turkey," when in reality, you need to stop talking to the person. Doing this is difficult, especially if you grew up being a people pleaser. You don't want to be viewed as the person who was only there to get the girl, or was only friends with the group because they wanted something from it. In reality, you expressed your emotions and saw the signs. It didn't work out how you thought it was going. Now you have to see the person you fell so deeply in love with as a friend. It's hard emotionally, and sometimes the best way to not have that emotion take center stage is by stepping away, not having something that reminds your brain of this person. Another scenario would be that they enjoy you as a friend because you've been putting in the attraction, putting in the work to keep the conversations alive. Now everything unfolded, and you are okay with being friends. The only difference is the effort both sides put in. You put in less effort, and they say you've changed. Yes, you have; you changed to keep your peace. There are two sides to yourself: one saying you are protecting your peace and the other saying you're not doing the right thing. I call these the new you and the old you. Yes, very creative. I know. The old you is trying to keep the peace; the new you keeps your own peace. You need to put your emotions above everyone else's, and if you can't heal from the reality of the moment, then it may be good for you to step away from this person.

All affection aside, throw the past in the garbage. If you met this person for the first time today, would you be friends with them? They want to be friends with you, but you wanted more. You viewed them differently, and now you have to view them how they view you. Is this person someone you can be friends with, or did you like them because they were attractive? That is the question you need to ask yourself. Getting hit with this is like starting over. Do you act the same around friends as you do with someone you like? You are starting over from square one, just with more information about the person. Ask yourself: is this person giving you energy that a friend should, or are you only holding on so you don't seem like that person? If they're giving you the energy, keep being friends with them. ## Chapter 8: The Tango, You Can't Dance Alone

## Chapter 15: Gratification, Now or Later, You Decide

Every decision you have ever made has brought you to this exact moment. The ones you are proud of and the ones you wish you could take back. All of them. And every decision you make from this moment forward is going to shape who you become next. There are always two options in front of you. The one that feels good right now. And the one that makes you better tomorrow. That choice (made over and over again across a lifetime) is the difference between the person you are and the person you can become. Just like anything in life, if we want to start something new, then a certain part of us has to die. Some people may have a life where they love everything they are doing and think they can just sneak in another challenge. Over time, you learn that at some point you are going to have to let something die. If you are hitting the gym and trying to have a good diet, the part that has to die in you is probably the fast food or snacking that you love. We all love doing it; nothing compares to just walking to the cabinet and grabbing a handful of snacks. Therefore, when you are looking to start something different, you must realize what is going to have to die in your current life. You are starting a business, maybe you have to give up what you do on weekends to focus on building the business that you have. It's tough to just let this part of you die. The brain doesn't like change, so it's going to test you in every way to go back to what it knows is comfortable. Trying to fight that urge is the second step in moving up the totem pole in your life.

When we think of gratification, there is a really great way to always think of it. That is from the form of delayed gratification. We live in a world where instant gratification is all over, and what we always want. You're tired from work and want to just lie down and let your mind decompress from everything that has happened during the day. That is instant gratification. When you lie down and watch a TV show or do the doom scrolling, are you going to remember and be excited about what you did the next day? Most likely not. The instant gratification is there, though. With simple errands, we push off until later because we are exhausted. Then later comes around, and we wish we had just done the errand because we are still exhausted, but it has to get done. Days like these come around all the time, and having a day where you take a complete mental reset is good for the brain. A day where you can just lie down and not constantly be moving. It is a rest day for the brain; most of us do not train seven days a week in the gym because we want our body to fully rest, sometimes the brain needs the same thing. When you feel burnt out, or feel that a lot of your energy is used to start a task that once was easy, it might be a good time to let your brain rest. The brain is wired to prioritize safety and predictability. We know that stepping out of safety and predictability is the first step in trying to achieve growth.

Life presents us with decisions, and many people ponder their choices at crucial moments. Do they want to go to that party? Most people will say yes because it sounds fun, offers entertainment for the night, and allows them to make memories. When thinking about the decision, is where we should understand delayed gratification. You'll get instant gratification from the party, but a rough morning with no motivation will follow. Here, the quick satisfaction you envisioned when contemplating the decision to go is now becoming apparent. I am not trying to convince you that going out to a party is a bad thing. Everyone has different values, but this is just one that I can relate to because it has happened to me a couple of times. I wasn't thinking about delayed gratification. When I went to the party and also had values on making progress on this book, those two clashed with each other. Where instant versus delayed gratification went neck and neck. Now I could've gone to the party and decided not to drink, leaving me refreshed the next morning. That was the happy medium. I wasn't thinking about that at the moment. Habits will stick out like a sore thumb: the ones that knock you out for days, or the ones where you get caught doomscrolling, letting the hours slip through your fingers. They know that you have goals to achieve; they know what is more important in your life, and they are your support system. Someone invites you to something that you have no business wanting to go but you have nothing else to do, the answer should still be no. That one million is sitting right in front of their face, and obviously, taking that one million will help them tremendously in the present. The instant gratification. We all started with nothing and worked our way up in life. From when we were infants to who we are now, every decision we have ever made has led us to this moment. Everyone carries a singular perspective and finds significance in their own unique way. The difference between those who are achieving goals and those who seem stuck is the choices they make today.

I saw a picture of the different zones that we put ourselves into. The picture divided the zones into four different categories, ranging from the comfort zone to the growth zone. First, is starting with the comfort zone, which is where one always wants to stay. The brain feels safe and in control of things, so it can't constantly be in flight or fight mode. Dribbling a basketball for the first time, basketball players probably sucked at it. Now they can run at full sprint, scan the floor, and decide without even thinking about dribbling. When we do something so many times, our brain classifies it as our comfort zone. Being in the comfort zone is the instant gratification that we find in ourselves every day. The things that we know will not delay our gratification for something better, but getting that instant shot of dopamine. Too much comfort zone can hinder the progress of building something. The comfort zone can fluctuate. It can be a curse because something that was once out of our comfort zone can now be in it. The next zone is the fear zone. The comfort and fear zones are the two zones that don't let us trend upward in life, but that doesn't mean that we can't live in them at all. The learning zone is what I think brings the fun to life itself. We are taking on new challenges and problems, so we are constantly learning something new, taking out the boredom in life. Why I mixed the two zones rather than talking about each one is that in the growth zone, we are setting the goals for the learning zone. ## Chapter 16: The Walls, Your Brain Draws the Circle

Somewhere along the way, your brain decided what you were capable of. It slowly built walls, growing larger and larger the more time passed. Not physical walls, but the emotional ones. The ones stopping you from applying for the job, starting the business, or having the conversation you've been avoiding. You built these walls unconsciously through every decision you made. Your brain understands what is comfortable and safe, so it blocks out the thing that's scary. The problem is safe and comfortable built nothing worth having. Your brain sets a ceiling on what you think you can do, and most people never question it. How far we can push in the gym, how long we can work on a certain project, or any of these kinds of thoughts. When the brain feels overwhelmed, it craves the comfort of familiar stuff. You are on a long run, and when your body gets tired, your mind tells you to stop or walk. Stretching your brain to reach outside of this "bubble" that I am explaining is a key to building a stronger and healthier life. Our brain has these walls that it will build on who we are, how we act, and how we view our life. There is a study that is shown all over social media, and it involves an ant and a spider. At the beginning of the study, they placed an ant on a piece of white paper. The ant roams and goes from every inch of the paper, and is basically free to go wherever it wants. Then they take the ant and place it back on the paper. They draw just a normal circle around the ant with a pen, and when the ant reaches the drawn circle, it is stuck. The ant's brain seems to think it is stuck in this circle, but obviously, when a human looks at the paper, they know the ant has all the power to just walk over the ink and move on with its day. Spider was the next animal that they did this study with, and the result was the same. The spider never crossed the line. The only difference between the spider and the ant was that the spider kept drawing circles smaller and smaller. As the person drew a smaller and smaller circle, the spider became convinced the area was shrinking. When the person drew the line directly beneath it, the spider instinctively crossed it. Once the spider crossed the line, it realized that the line wasn't a wall that it couldn't pass, so it just roamed off the paper. The person drawing the walls repeatedly tried to trap the spider, but the spider recognized the pen was no longer a wall barrier and fearlessly crossed the line.

The spider and ant kind of relate to how a lot of us see the world. We believe we are trapped inside this bubble, and our brain prevents us from expanding our knowledge. You're too old to try something; you don't have the resources; you don't have the skills to do the thing; all excuses to make you feel good. The only excuse we should have is that we tried, and it wasn't for me. When you expand your knowledge and push out of your comfort zone, then you realize that the imaginary walls that your brain put up are just imaginary, and you can complete what you once thought was so daunting. Perhaps you are trying to write a book, or you want to ask someone a question, or you are scared to try an exercise in the gym. We all have these imaginary walls that get built up because the brain wants comfort. That is what it is born to do: seek comfort. Once you realize that many of your fears in life are just your brain instilling them into your thoughts, you branch outside of your own comfort zone and complete a lot more fun and interesting activities that you once thought you could never do. The best way to not have any fear is by just doing the thing.

In the bubble of our brain, there are a couple of things that get in our way. The first thing is our fear. We live in our own world, and we want to always be comfortable with our lives. Always have the job that gives you an income, always drive the car that is going to keep us from getting looked at funny, or have the house that makes people respect you. All of this is just a situation where you don't want your image to be looked at. You are living in fear of validation from others. Your lifestyle does not match your income. You wonder why you are living paycheck to paycheck, but then you look at your lifestyle, and it only leaves you with a couple of pennies each paycheck. Leaving your failure point solely relying on your job. No wonder you fear branching off and trying to start something you want. You don't have that safety net to give yourself time to start something. Now you don't need the safety net all the time; if you need money, you will find it somewhere. The second thing that gets in our way is belief. Our belief in what we can achieve is great. Many people will have the emotions that they are not enough or that they are not talented enough to go for their goal. This kind of belief is something that will beat you down for the rest of your life if you don't change the way you think. We all have some sort of courage built up inside of us. Courage is like a muscle; the more we train it, the stronger it will get. Every time you do something that frightens you, you realize at the end how your mind was playing tricks about how scary it actually was. Then, over time, things that once frightened us are just another day in our lives. We want to build up our courage for ourselves, and then you will see your beliefs change to something that will make the walls look a lot smaller than they used to. The third thing is disruptive emotions. Our emotions are a big turning point in trying to achieve the boundaries of our brain. If you go into every challenge with a defeating emotion and get angry at every little roadblock that gets in your way, then you can never move forward in your life. You will constantly fight internal battles, and you will spend most of your energy attempting to motivate yourself to complete a task. A recipe for disaster. The way out is not to fight the emotions; one thing we can't do is control our emotions. But we can understand them, recognize when they come up, name them, and then ask yourself if acting on this emotion is the right choice or not. That brief pause for reflection is the difference between responding and reacting. Habits are the fourth thing. The routines we repeat daily either expand our bubble or shrink it. We covered this in depth earlier, but know that your habits are quietly drawing a circle around you every single day. The fifth thing is missing a skill. We all have skills we can build in life. When you were young, someone taught you how to ride a bike. Maybe you started with training wheels and then finally could go on two wheels. That was a skill that we learned; we didn't just have that skill at birth. Which is the same for any other aspect of your life. If we want a skill, we have to acquire it. Elon Musk wasn't the smartest person at age 8. He was developing skills throughout his life, which made him one of the smartest people at building different businesses in his life. Think of something you started, but then stopped. However long ago, imagine how much better you would have been if you stuck with the thing? You would've built the skill to be great at the thing. Writing has never been my strong suit in life. It still isn't my strongest suit, but the more and more I write, the better my word selection in sentences becomes. Over time, I know it will only get better; sentences will be more engaging, and the word selection will flow better. If you think to yourself that you are not smart enough to start a business, then you are already failing. Having this kind of thought covers all five of the things that I just mentioned. You have the fear that you are not smart enough; you don't have the belief that you are smart enough to even try, your emotions are taking control of your body, only bad habits are being introduced in this mindset, and you are not even going to get the skill you need to start it. These different things are controlling our lives; we want to control our own lives.

## Chapter 17: The Curse, Your Greatest Strength Might Be Your Biggest Obstacle

You pride yourself on being tough. On handling things others can't. But have you ever stopped to ask what that strength is actually costing you? You set the standards high, ones that you think once you reach in life, you will be happy, fulfilled, and finally able to settle down. Well, I wish it were that easy. Setting the standards high can have its own curse, too. You are good at things; you set your standards to the height of those things. Now you know you should be able to at least reach the level of the standards that you have set. Your standards are so high that anything short of reaching them feels like failure. The reasonable level of performance for most people becomes your floor, not your ceiling. And if you fall below your own standard, even slightly, your brain registers it as a loss. You are in a success or failure mindset. The small achievements you've made while pursuing your goals are now being set aside. Your enjoyment of completing small things is now way out the window; you're set on the end goal. Anything less than success itself would be a failure. Now the success you are reaching for is the least you can accept, missing the little wins along the way. You have now achieved success and reached the top of your standards, accomplishing everything you set out to do. Congratulations, although now you may be miserable. I say this because you didn't enjoy the process, didn't enjoy the small moments you had, the setbacks. You beat yourself up over the constant thought of not achieving your goal, so you are a failure. Once again, congratulations.

Here's something the insecure overachiever never stops to consider. You never got to choose the circumstances you started with. Not your family, not your resources, not your environment. You are adapting with what you have, in the world you were placed in. So when you look at someone else succeeding and wonder why you can't get there, remember, they may have started on a completely different playing field. Your success isn't slower because you're less capable. It's slower because the path is longer. Our ancestors, who were so goal-driven in life, to make a better purpose of the family tree, have always had this curse on them. The insecure overachiever who is always trying to be more and more driven by their accomplishments than just living. We look back at our ancestors, and their success looked a lot different from ours today. They wanted food and resources. If they had food on the table and a good supply of wood for the fire, maybe even a shelter over their head, they felt successful. Nowadays, in the world we live in, our measure of success is specifically on accomplishments and money. Every single one of us is chasing the next dollar. When exactly will someone have "enough" money? We look at Bezos or Elon musk and you see their net worth, they could feed generations upon generations of families. I mean, it is just insane. Everybody views them as successful. Elon Musk himself was even asked how he does it, what his thought process is, and his reply to the question, "I do not want anyone ever to experience the thoughts that go on inside my head." I can't put into words exactly what goes into his head, but do I think he is an insecure overachiever? Absolutely. He sold PayPal, then started another company in Tesla, then bought another company as what we know as X or Twitter. He is constantly chasing this status for himself. Insecure overachievers' brains just do not care about how they feel; it only wants to be successful. All of us have this drive within us; some are more severe than others, but just take the time to look back on your accomplishments. No matter how big or small, see the work you are currently putting in and admire it. Then, once you are content with where you currently are, keep chasing that dream. Do not be miserable in the chase for success; once you get to the end, you will look back and understand how miserable you were in the process and how miserable you still feel, even though you may have all the success in the world. If you don't enjoy the small moments, life will just move by you in the blink of an eye. Leaving you to miss so much more because of tunnel vision. The insecure overachiever and the emotionally enduring person have the same traits. They push through challenges, never celebrating the small wins, and then wonder why success doesn't feel like enough.

Emotional endurance can have its own curse. We all have a limit to what we do. Whether it's physical or emotional. How far we can run or how much pain someone can put you through before you finally decide to make a move. Although we view psychological strength as a good thing, it has its own downside. You are the person who can handle hard times, ones where others would fold and retreat. The drive that you built is shaping your life; you can ignore your feelings and keep progressing. That drive helps build your career and brand. When your psychological strength is the same as a gorilla's physical strength, our goal is to figure things out. No matter how tough they feel, you take all the challenges in your life and make them work. You've trained yourself that struggle and difficulty are challenges, and you won't back away from a challenge. So you try to make everything work out. Let's say you are in the dating scene or in a relationship, and you are just not feeling connected, or someone says that they don't like you. The psychological side of you won't let that happen; it won't want to leave. It wants to fight; it sees a challenge. Now you are trying to figure out a challenge that is solved by just leaving, but you can't. You want to prove to yourself that you can get through anything. You learned your needs are irrelevant unless you prove your worth. In love, you have to prove yourself. Within this mindset, we push people who are open and ready to love us. The brain doesn't see a challenge, so it wants no part of it. Then, once it sees something that is difficult and disconnected, it latches onto it. We attempt to force something that resists being forced.

Having this mindset hinders your ability to understand what you want and what you don't want. You pass by things that are not a challenge because not all of them will be a challenge. The girl/man who is ready and open to love you, you just look by. The thing that you want is a healthy relationship, but since it's easy, you look straight past it. We also see that these types of people have a tendency to hide their own emotions because they don't want to make us look weak. Their mantra is always 'my feelings don't matter,' then they wonder why they can never connect with their feelings. That disconnection is exactly what vulnerability fixes.

We have all heard someone say something along the lines of "Don't show your emotions because it makes you weak." Yeah, that's the case in some situations. Don't look weak to a bear; they will eat that up. We live in this world where we want to be the hardest person, and that makes us think people are going to be intimidated by us. A quote from Oliver Burkman: "Outward complaints are not a good gauge of internal suffering. Just because someone carries it well doesn't mean it isn't heavy." Running from all the thoughts that could spark sadness, or when someone asks you how you are actually feeling, you always say I'm fine. Not showing that side of you that just wants to let everything out. You are holding it all in well, but in reality, you are so miserable inside. Chris Williamson put it perfectly in a blog I came across: "What looks like strength from the outside becomes self-abandonment on the inside." Vulnerability is saying things that are true, even if it scares us. You're experiencing a difficult period in your life, and you tell someone about it. While you don't want to burden the person you're speaking with, it's the truth. You are showcasing what you are actually feeling, not just running from them. Waiting for them to catch you down the road, but even stronger. Chris Williamson raised the question, "Who is stronger. Someone who can feel their feelings fully and express them, or the one who flees the scene when the emotion gets too close." We will all have different answers to this question, but let me give my answer. I think the stronger person is the one who can sit with their feelings fully. Understanding what sparks general emotion and being able to guide through life as the same person is what I see as a strength. The person who flees from their emotions isn't strong because they are going to have to get a simple pleasure to run from it. Whether that is some alcohol so their mind can get a little groggy and everything becomes good again, or the person who is always trying to be the toughest in the room. I don't see strength in that; I see someone who is hoping that life is just going to become better with no emotional regulation.

When you think of your life. What is your main purpose? Mine is to live it fully. Living through the ups and downs, the laughs, the cries, being able to look back and understand that everything that was thrown my way has a purpose, a lesson. Vulnerability allows us to do just that. Instead of suppressing our emotions, detaching from them, running from what life is actually meant to be while something inside of ourselves is quietly breaking. So when we have a friend, family member, or even just a stranger who might open up to us. Explaining their problems, how they are struggling through a time, or even a thrilling moment they are feeling. When they are opening up, listen; do not sit there and try to fix their lives. Only one person can do that, and maybe they are trying, but the burden just needs to be shared in their eyes. Be the person who sits in the mud with them. If they ask for advice, then give them advice. Showing vulnerability is not a weakness characteristic, but in fact a strong characteristic showing emotional intelligence.

We can tell ourselves we are tough. Psychologically unbreakable. But sometimes the strongest thing you can do is drop the walls entirely. Psychological strength is a gift; until it isn't. You see a challenge, and you don't complain about the pain; you keep pushing through regardless of the emotions you are feeling. Remember, you don't need to have something be a challenge in order to trust it. Psychological strength doesn't always make us strong; sometimes it makes us struggle through times we don't have to. Meaning we stay longer in situations because they feel like a challenge. No one is going to congratulate you for taking on challenges that you never needed to. Understand things in your life that are not meant to be a challenge, one of them being your relationships, and don't use your psychological strength on them. Let it go; stop fighting for a challenge that isn't right for you. Don't get hit with the curse of having a challenger's mind. Use it to your advantage, know your limits, and keep pushing on things that matter.

A very hard task because social events, family gatherings, and all events are normally centered on drinking alcohol. So you show up to one of these events and your friends say, "Come on, just one drink", "You're so lame now", these different sayings trying to better yourself.

As we talked about earlier, with delayed gratification, we need to keep that in mind. Every day, we need to make a decision that is going to move us toward our goals. As we have already gone over, slow in the correct direction is better than fast in the wrong direction. Learning who you are is a great step in the lonely chapter; a bunch of different thoughts about yourself loom in the air. For me, during these times, I have been able to understand where these thoughts come from, what exactly makes me happy, and also what my values in life are. I have learned so much about myself during this chapter, and I think it is something people need to go through to understand who they really are. I want to go into the example where I learned who I really am. When I first realized I was slowly getting into this lonely chapter setting in my life, I had the same thoughts that I have mentioned above. Like the two boxers boxing one another, on which side I wanted to go. The old habits were slowly creeping into my head, my brain wanting to find comfort, and the motivation that I once had started to slowly fade away. I had a purpose, a goal in mind, knowing what I wanted to do and knowing that this phase of my life would one day be the reason I am enjoying the larger moments, so I kept fighting. Having that purpose and understanding what delayed gratification was, I can consistently try to get better every day. Even when the loneliest of nights set in, trying to get 1% better every day was my reason. So if you are currently going through this chapter in your life, it is normal. This is a chapter every person has to go through because you are evolving. Hatching out of that old shell of yours like a caterpillar turning into a butterfly. It's a chapter that no one likes to go through, but one that is so key to building ourselves up, understanding who we are, and seeing if we really have the drive for the goals we want to achieve.

That is the lonely chapter. Days feel lonelier than ever, and it is so easy to just go back to what made you happy. Where we see who has the true strength to hatch from their own shells or go right back into the comfortable shell. The only person who can get you through this chapter is yourself; you need to rely on who you are in the moment and understand that your goal is much larger than who you are. When people critique you, tell you how to do the thing, just remember that you shouldn't take advice from someone who has never walked in your shoes.

## Chapter 25: Stress vs Yourself, Use It or Let It Use You

Think about the last time you were really stressed. Not the small type of stress, the kind that sits on your chest and follows you from when that alarm clock rings to your head hitting the pillow. You were probably trying to do too much, falling behind on things that mattered, and telling yourself it will get better; you just have to get through it. Getting through it kept feeling further away, leaving you questioning everything. The thing about stress, you are not supposed to eliminate it. You are supposed to use it. We often throw around the word stress in our everyday lives. "I am so stressed at my job, they are making me do so much at one time," or "I am stressed out because I have to run the kids to practice, then get home and make dinner, then this and that," the list never ends. When we stress, we make things worse than they may seem. Think of a moment that you didn't want to do at all; maybe it is even reading a couple of pages in a book. You think of many other things that you can do rather than read a book. I have done it a million times. Stress is going to happen in our lives; there is no way to just eliminate stress. There is a way to deal with stress. Stress will destroy you or drive you to become the best version of yourself. As people, we are made to create. Create our lives. When we create our lives, focus on what we can control, and then the stress disappears. Not because the stress is gone, but because we can control and push through past stressful times. We want stress to drive us in our lives, but not let it control us.

Whether that is just as simple as a daily task, or if we look at something larger, like starting a business or starting a side project that you never started, or started on a day but never finished because your life is too busy. Three things will happen when we keep pushing through stressful times. The first is the most obvious: we will find out how strong you really are. When we stretch ourselves to limits that we possibly don't think are possible, we transform our brains to focus on more challenging things. Telling ourselves about the delayed gratification of life is more important than the present gratification. Stressful times could be so small as well, like daily tasks that you know will make you better, but not a single ounce of you wants to do them, like journaling or a daily Bible scripture. Pushing through those moments will reveal how strong you are, and then you can walk around with confidence you never even thought you had. Second, you find out who your real friends are. I talked earlier about your friend group, but when you push through stressful times with certainty, then you will see who either comes with you to cheer you on, or tells you to go back to your old ways. Third, immunity. You will become immune to the small things that used to stress you out, such as a traffic jam, a slow walker on the sidewalk, or a person annoying you. You know those little things take up so much energy in your life that you have bigger fish to stress about. Now you will be stronger, more confident, and smarter, just because you have built this structure in your mind that you know can start tackling anything.

The real growth happens after you push through. That's when the patterns become visible. Pattern recognition is a major key in learning when reflecting on the stress that you caused yourself. Pattern recognition is when we recognize different events in our lives. We can look at pattern recognition through the scope of life, so it doesn't just have to be through our own emotions. When we recognize the past patterns of our lives, it will free us from the fear we might have. You will notice the little subtle cues that go off in your brain and understand what is about to happen. When we master the power of pattern recognition in our brain, then we become a stronger version of ourselves, one that can go through stress with their head up and shoulders back, as Jordan Peterson would say. Now, let's dive into patterns that will help us get out of the rut we may be in. The first pattern that we may notice is how we talk to ourselves. Saying I can't, or this is just who I am. We want to stop that dead in its tracks and change that. This kind of language will keep you stuck. It's okay to say you have engaged with a bad pattern; that pattern is now in the past. We engaged with it, but it is not who you are anymore; it's who you were. People will always come up with a story on why they can't improve or are not enough for a certain thing. When in reality it's just them making up that story, engraving that pattern that they are not enough in their own brain. Every human's deepest fear is that they are not enough. When I was listening to Tony Robbins, he mentioned something that quite changed the way I looked at myself. Our bodies change completely after only two years. A new layer replaces our skin's current layer every 2 to 4 weeks. Our cells in our body get replaced. I don't exactly know the number, but everything in our body will get replaced in two years. The only thing that doesn't get replaced? Our memory. Our memory stays with us as long as we want it to. We can also change our memory simply by rewiring how we speak to ourselves. "This is not who I am. I may have previous patterns, but that is not who I am." Then, over time, we will see small changes. Just like I mentioned about habits, we want small changes that will compound over the days and weeks, and then you will be a completely different person. The second pattern we see people stress over is finding their purpose. I am guilty as charged with this one. I always stressed repeatedly about what kind of purpose I might have in my life, and when I was sitting there with zero purpose, I was stressing just trying to find the holy grail of purpose. Who says that we need to have only one purpose in life? I know no one does, but we sure think so. When we want to find a purpose, we can't get stuck in the mud with no movement. When you have some movement, maybe it's just even going to the gym for a better health habit, we have a purpose in our lives. Then that one purpose can start branching off into other purposes. We have so much undue stress just trying to find the holy grail or ultimate purpose that we cannot realize that progress is everything. If you're making progress in your life, you will be happy. Focus on the results of your goals. Asking "What do I need to do" differs from "What do I want to do?" What I want to do might not get you the results you want, but it's what you want to do in the moment: instant vs. delayed gratification. What you need to do will get you results, and results lead to progress, and progress leads to happiness. It all flows down the same river, and it keeps flowing with every decision you make.

Let me ask you a question. Do you want to make everything perfect in your life? Are you a perfectionist in the things you do? I think you see where I'm going with the third pattern. Perfectionism. Perfectionism is the lowest standard a human being can have of themselves. Think about it. When you wait until everything is perfect before you start, you are really just afraid to fail. You care so much about the outcome that you never take the first step. What successful person has ever had a perfect plan or a perfect life? We all have ups and downs, and life will never be perfect. Nothing about it will allow you to be perfect, so why do we have the perfectionist mindset to have the perfect plan before doing anything? I was stuck in the perfectionist loop, and I started nothing I wanted to because of it. We set impossible goals that keep us from doing the thing. Focus on results and progress. Don't focus on the perfect plan; you will learn along the way. The best way to learn is to fail; to fail, you must try. Thinking about the plan isn't trying. When we try to be perfect for other people (to look right, say the right things, never make mistakes) we slowly lose ourselves. We are all messy people living messy lives. Chasing perfection means abandoning who you actually are and replacing it with performance. When you abandon yourself to meet someone else's standard, yours sits at the very bottom; the lowest standard.

Perfection lowers our energy, which causes us to get emotionally stuck inside our own heads, leading to stress. If progress makes us happy, how will sitting back and getting the perfect plan ever get us happy? It won't. A fourth pattern in stressed individuals is their lack of action. Kind of piggybacking off of perfectionism because they mainly coexist with each other. When we don't have any action, then our emotions are the thing in control of our lives. When emotions are in control of our lives, and we have no progress, where are you going to get positive emotions from? Therefore, your feelings do not matter; the actions that you take in everyday life matter most. When we think of someone who is in a down state, what is the picture you paint in our heads? Shoulders down, head down, and dragging our feet. The state we are in is terrible. Our actions control the state we are in, and the state we are in controls the thoughts we have in our heads. That is how it should be: head up and shoulders back. See how everything connects? If your feelings lead, you will be screwed. Take control of your feelings by taking action. Just as progress leads to happiness, action leads to control. The fifth pattern that we see is that people carry around their old stories. Have you ever tried to get someone to quit something, and you get the terrible excuse of "This is just who I am." That person is carrying around their old story because they think they can't change. People go to therapy, and you talk about why you are the way you are. Great therapists try to reinforce your brain to cut out your old patterns and rewire your brain to start a new life. Think of a CD. When you scratch the crap out of a part of the CD, you lose functionality to a part of a song, or a movie, or whatever's on the CD. Our brain is the CD. Anytime that we get a new belief, we want to get rid of the old belief. If we want to add something new, we must replace something bad. So you take the CD of your brain and scratch it as hard as you can, causing it to malfunction when you go to use it. We want the old beliefs to leave our brains and stop working. Then have the new beliefs, hopefully the good ones, take over our brains and rewire ourselves into a new version. Every time you tell yourself you are the old story, all you are doing is planting the seed for it to stay longer.

I like these 5 patterns because each one offers a unique perspective on the causes of stress, and we all incorporate elements of each pattern into our personalities. The patterns all weave together because a lot of us are trying to find our purpose in our lives. When I look at these patterns, the main thing that I took away from it all is that we need to take action in our lives. Action leaves us with progress. When we have progress, we put ourselves in a good state; when we put ourselves in a good state, our thoughts get put into a good state. All leading to a life that we want to live. Action is the solution to almost anything, and the best time to start was 20 years ago; the second-best time to start is now.

## Chapter 26: Time, Make It Worth Remembering

Blink. Another week has gone by. Blink again. You are graduating. Blink a third time. You have a family. Time is flying, and you didn't mean for it to go that fast; it just did. You probably can't remember most of it. That's not an accident. When your brain doesn't have to react, think, or do anything new, it stops recording. Your days become forgettable. And forgettable days become a forgettable life.

We hear people say how fast time flies, especially at the end of the year. I have a small theory on why time flies the older you get. When we were young and in school, with the schedule of holiday breaks and days off, we always knew how far away we were. The countdown to the last day of school or the countdown to final exams that are taking place. Every day, you had to be aware of what was coming soon: the test or exam, the project due date, or when the homecoming dance was. We were always on a certain time clock, where we had some time off and got to enjoy it as kids. So throughout the many years of us in school, we were aware of exactly how our weeks were unfolding. In the working world, you get a certain number of PTO days. Other than the holidays, when your work gives you time off. As an employee, you get to use your PTO days however you want. People normally always align their vacations in March during the week of spring break. Well, guess what now? You can take a vacation in mid-October, and it would be the same price as if you were to take it in March. The school schedule's internal clock, which once lived in our brains, has now completely reverted to whatever event is happening this week. Another theory that I have about time speeding up as we progress through our lives is just by the age we are currently at. When we were kids, a year seemed like forever. We only lived for five full years. The concept of time differed completely from when we were 30 years old. When we are 30, obviously, a year seems a lot shorter because we have lived through 29 full years and now fully understand the concept of time.

How many of us have gone through a year and looked back on it all and wondered how it all went by so fast? In between Christmas and New Year's, you reflect on where the entire year has taken you, saying to yourself, "Wow, this year really flew by. I don't even remember most of it." Years are merging with even days. We can't really recall how you spent all your time. Months feel like days, and days feel like minutes. The passage of time is moving faster than we expect it to; we feel like observers of our own lives. Not the ones controlling it. You may already know this, but let me re-add it into your brain. No matter how boring the party is, how fun the vacation you are taking in another country is, or how old you are. Time still passes at the same rate for all of us. A minute is still sixty seconds, and a day comprises 24 hours. So, why do we think some years fly by faster than others? I like to view it as a moment of the present time and the remembered time. The present time is the physical seconds ticking through the actual day. It will always remain at the same speed. Our remembered time could vary widely. When we think about a month and say that the month went by so quickly, what we are actually saying to ourselves is that we recall little that happened in the month. When we say that we had a long week, it is normally the opposite. We remember all the things that we did, whether it's all the things at work, all of the extracurricular activities, or just life feels like a boulder.

So, how does remembered time vary across all aspects of time? First, the brain stores memories in three different ways. Sensory memory, which is the immediate senses in your body (sight, sound, touch), only lasts for seconds. Can you recall how your lunch smelled three days ago? Unless it was an incredible lunch that differed from your routine, you probably don't. Short-term memory relies on the prefrontal cortex. This is the memory that normally lasts for minutes in our brains. Someone gives you a number to remember or a task to do. Our brains will hold onto that memory for a couple of minutes, then it will go right out the window. Then, the long-term memory. The memory that varies our remembered time the most. Two different ways to look at long-term memory. Implicit long-term memory is the first aspect, which is the skills and habits that you learn. How to ride a bike, how to write on paper, etc. Implicit memory is handled by the basal ganglia and cerebellum. Explicit memory is the other side of long-term memory. Explicit memory is the facts and personal events that we store in memory. The vacation that we went on, or the random fact you know and no one else does. Our brain's hippocampus and neocortex store these memories. Our brain stores memories of events that are intense or new to it. I bet you remember the first time driving a car, exactly what car it was, what route you took, exactly who you were with, but you ask yourself about four days ago, you probably don't even remember how you got to work. So our brain is constantly looking for comfort because that is what it always wants. When times get intense, or something new happens in the brain, it records your surroundings. Arriving at a hotel on vacation, you may remember exactly what the person at the counter was wearing. People like to call it the "Holiday Paradox." Time flies when you're having fun, but in retrospect, it felt like it was a long time. How our remembered time versus our present time differs. Having the same commute every day, seeing the same people, and doing the same work can make it seem that time is flying by. When in reality, it is the same time you took that vacation last month.

When we get older, time seems to fly by much more quickly. There are a couple of reasons I think this might be the case. First, we reflect on the remembered time and exactly how it works. When we get older, our memories of moments and time become fewer. Mainly because they have experienced more things, and new moments are scarcer. So we see the brain not capturing and recording these moments, making our remembered time looking back more of a blur rather than a time we remember. The hard truth is, as we get older, our days go by more quickly because we can't remember them, meaning we have done nothing memorable with our days. Our days are forgettable; we forget them. How can we slow down the passage of time? We need to say yes to experiences that are new or eventful to us and start saying no to the events we are not excited to go to. Putting more novelty and experiences into your life will help you understand the passage of time, and when you look back, you will have many more memories, making it feel like your days are not just one big blur. It's hard for us to go out and do new things; we want to just stay at home and watch Netflix because it doesn't take much effort to do. Will you remember your day months from now if you watch another movie or if you go out and do something new? Ask yourself what memories you can take away from today. The more you can answer that question, the more your days, months, or years will feel filled rather than all blended.
